Climate Patterns and Air Pollution in Australia

Exploring the relationship between temperature, rainfall, humidity and PM2.5 levels across Australian states.

Overview

Australia’s diverse climate plays a crucial role in shaping its air quality. The country’s vast north–south span creates sharp contrasts—from the tropical heat of the Northern Territory to the cooler southern coastlines. These climatic differences not only define how people experience weather, but also determine how pollutants form, disperse, and persist in the atmosphere.

Northern Australia stays consistently warmer than the south. Higher temperatures in NT and QLD set the stage for higher pollution risk when winds are calm.

PM2.5 hotspots appear in the north and west. Cooler and wetter states such as TAS and VIC show the lowest annual PM2.5 levels.

While the scatter plots reveal no strong linear correlation between PM2.5 and individual weather factors, a general tendency emerges — warmer and drier regions (such as the Northern Territory and Western Australia) record higher PM2.5 levels, whereas cooler, wetter states (like Tasmania and Victoria) maintain cleaner air. This suggests that climate sets the environmental background for pollution dispersion, even if other local factors (such as emissions and wind) also play key roles.

Conclusion

2024 data shows a clear spatial and climatic pattern for air quality in Australia. Warmer northern states tend to experience higher annual PM2.5, while cooler and wetter southern states remain cleaner. Most states still sit above the WHO annual guideline of 5 µg/m³. These findings suggest two priorities: (1) reduce emissions and dust sources during hot, dry periods; and (2) coordinate climate adaptation with air‑quality management — early warnings during heat and drought, more green/blue infrastructure, and regional controls around high‑risk seasons. Together, these actions can lower exposure and protect public health.
